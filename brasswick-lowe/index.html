01What we help with

Four situations we work on.

If yours isn’t on the list, tell us what the letter says and we’ll tell you whether it’s something we handle.

  • A

    A notice or letter you don’t understand

    We read every page, tell you what the agency is saying and what it wants back, and draft the reply if one is needed.

  • B

    Returns you haven’t filed

    One year or several. We gather the income records that exist, rebuild what’s missing, and prepare the returns in order.

  • C

    A balance you can’t pay at once

    We go through the payment arrangements the agency offers, work out what your budget can carry, and prepare and submit the request.

  • D

    An audit or a request for records

    We organize what’s being asked for, respond in writing on your behalf, and keep you posted on every letter that comes back.

What we won’t tell you

That a balance will go away, or how your case will end, before we’ve read the file. Nobody can honestly say that on a first call. What we can do is review it, explain where you stand, and do the paperwork properly.

02How it works

Four steps, in the same order every time.

  1. 1

    You send us the notice

    A phone photo of every page is fine. We reply within one business day to set up a first conversation.

  2. 2

    We read it and check the record

    With your signed permission, we request your account records from the agency so we’re working from their numbers, not guesses.

  3. 3

    We lay out your options

    You get a short written summary: what each option involves, what it costs to prepare, and what we’d do in your place. You decide.

  4. 4

    We handle the paperwork

    We prepare the forms, send the replies, and stay on the correspondence until the matter is closed. You get a copy of everything.

03Your first conversation

What to have in front of you.

The first conversation is about understanding the letter, not judging how you got here. Bring what you can find.

Missing half of this? Come anyway. Finding the rest is part of the job.

  • The notice itselfEvery page, front and back. Note the reply date printed on it.
  • Earlier letters on the same matterEven ones you didn’t open.
  • The last return you filedWhatever year that was.
  • Income records you still haveW-2s, 1099s, bank statements. Incomplete is normal.
  • A rough monthly budgetOnly if the question is how to pay a balance.
  • Your questionsWrite them down first. People forget them on the call.
